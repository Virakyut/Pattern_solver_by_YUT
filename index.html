How to solve these by hand

  1. Find the differences between consecutive terms. All equal → arithmetic, done.
  2. If not constant, take differences of the differences. Constant at that level → a 2nd‑order pattern, often related to squares.
  3. Check the ratios (each term divided by the one before). Constant ratio → geometric.
  4. Try a rule like ×a + b. Solve for a and b from the first three terms, then check it holds for the rest.
  5. Split into odd and even positions. Many sequences are two simple patterns woven together.
  6. Check if each term is the sum of the previous two or three (Fibonacci-style).
  7. Compare to known families: squares, cubes, primes.
